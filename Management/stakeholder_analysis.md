## All Stakeholders (Original Four + Additional)

**Originally identified:**
- Fans (End Users)
- Brands & Sponsors
- Venues & Stadiums
- Brisbane 2032 OCOG

**Additional stakeholders worth naming:**
- TransLink / Transport & Infrastructure Providers
- Local Queensland Tech/SME Suppliers (build partner)
- Athletes & National Sporting Federations
- State & Federal Government (Queensland Government, Australian Government)
- Concession & Merchandise Vendors
- Australian Privacy Regulators (OAIC)
- Competing Fan Engagement Platforms (Monterosa, LiveLike, etc.)
- Post-2032 Licensees (AFL, NRL, Cricket Australia, A-League)

---

## Power/Interest Matrix

### High Power, High Interest → Manage Closely
- **Brisbane 2032 OCOG** — controls venue access, Wi-Fi infrastructure, official partner status; deeply invested in fan experience outcomes and legacy
- **Venues / Stadiums (Queensland Stadiums)** — control the commercial relationship, own the sponsor relationships we depend on
- **Brands & Sponsors** — control the revenue; high interest because they're paying for measurable engagement

**What this means:** These three need continuous, proactive engagement — regular reporting, involvement in key decisions, and a seat at the table. Losing any one of them stalls the whole model. Most of the team's energy and communication effort should go here.

---

### High Power, Low Interest → Keep Satisfied
- **State & Federal Government** — has significant influence (funding, regulation, Olympic mandate) but isn't focused on our specific platform day-to-day
- **Australian Privacy Regulators (OAIC)** — high power to block or restrict if non-compliant, but not actively monitoring unless triggered
- **TransLink / Infrastructure Providers** — control critical infrastructure but aren't emotionally invested in our success

**What this means:** Don't over-communicate, but stay compliant and visible enough that they don't become blockers. A light-touch, periodic check-in relationship — make sure they're aware of us and have no reason for concern.

---

### Low Power, High Interest → Keep Informed
- **Fans (End Users)** — no formal power over business decisions, but the entire value proposition depends on their genuine engagement
- **Athletes & National Sporting Federations** — care about how their sport is represented, but don't control commercial terms
- **Concession & Merchandise Vendors** — benefit directly from the model, motivated to see it succeed, but limited influence over strategy
- **Local Tech/SME Supplier (build partner)** — interested and engaged in delivery, but power sits with whoever commissions the build (OCOG/venues)
- **Post-2032 Licensees (AFL, NRL, Cricket Australia, A-League)** — currently latent, but highly interested once the platform proves itself; little power until the licensing conversation begins

**What this means:** This group should receive regular updates and feedback channels — fan engagement data, in particular, becomes the proof point that converts high-power stakeholders. Vendors, federations, and future licensees are useful allies and amplifiers worth nurturing, even though they don't make the final commercial calls.

---

### Low Power, Low Interest → Monitor
- **Competing Fan Engagement Platforms (Monterosa, LiveLike, etc.)** — not actively involved in the project, no power over decisions, but their market moves are worth tracking

**What this means:** No active engagement needed, but periodic competitive monitoring is worthwhile — if a competitor moves into the Australian market, this stakeholder could shift quadrants quickly.
