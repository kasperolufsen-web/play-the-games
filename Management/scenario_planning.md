# Scenario Planning — Play the Games

## Step 1 | Focus Question

**Focus Question:**
In light of rapid digital transformation in fan engagement, what is our response to the potential longer-term implications for sponsor-funded fan attention in Australian sport?

**Time Horizon:** 6–7 years (2027–2034)

This covers the full Play the Games rollout — from the 2027 Queensland Stadiums pilot through to Brisbane 2032 and the post-Games licensing phase.

---

## Step 2 | Critical Influences

### Why These Two Critical Influences?

Following the prioritisation logic of impact × uncertainty, several candidate dimensions were tested before settling on the final matrix.

**Dimensions considered but not selected:**

- *Technology Adoption Speed × Regulatory Environment* — relevant to feasibility, but largely manageable; not the make-or-break uncertainty
- *Major Event Pipeline × Economic Conditions* — important for revenue timing, but more a scheduling factor than a structural uncertainty about whether the model works
- *Venue Digital Maturity × Brand Risk Appetite* — a more granular version of the chosen axes, harder to communicate clearly
- *Olympic Legacy Centralisation × Code Fragmentation* — relevant post-2032, but too narrow for the full horizon
- *Consumer Trust in Data Exchange × Competitive Landscape* — relevant to social licence, but secondary to whether fans want to engage at all

**Why Fan Attention Migration × Commercial Investment Appetite wins:**

These two influences sit furthest outside our control, carry the highest business impact, and directly answer our "How Might We." Every other variable — technology, regulation, event timing, competition — only matters *if* fans are willing to put their phones down and *if* someone is willing to pay for it. These two uncertainties are the foundation everything else is built on.

### Axis 1: Fan Attention Migration
*The degree to which fans shift their dead-time attention from social media to in-venue engagement*

| Extreme | Description |
|---|---|
| Social Media Stays Dominant | Fans continue defaulting to TikTok/Instagram during dead time; in-venue behaviour is unchanged |
| In-Venue Engagement Becomes the Norm | Fans actively prefer playing on the stadium screen over scrolling their own |

### Axis 2: Commercial Investment Appetite
*The willingness of sponsors and venues to fund and adopt interactive in-venue formats*

| Extreme | Description |
|---|---|
| Conservative, Traditional Sponsorship | Sponsors stick to proven formats (billboards, jersey logos); venues are slow to upgrade infrastructure |
| Aggressive Investment in Interactive Formats | Sponsors actively seek new engagement formats; venues fast-track digital infrastructure upgrades |

## Step 3 | Scenario Frameworks

```

### Scenario A — "Full Stadium, Full Screen"
**Fan attention shifts strongly in-venue + sponsors invest aggressively**

**What's happening:** Fans actively prefer the stadium screen over their own phone during dead time. Sponsors compete for game slots. Venues race to upgrade Wi-Fi and screen infrastructure to keep up with demand.

**Impact on us:** Best-case scenario. Demand outpaces our rollout plan — the risk shifts from "will this work" to "can we scale fast enough."

**What we'd need to do:**
- Accelerate venue partnerships beyond the original Phase 2 timeline
- Lock in exclusivity agreements with key venues before competitors enter the space
- Fast-track licensing conversations with AFL, NRL, and Cricket Australia ahead of schedule
- Invest early in cross-venue leaderboard infrastructure to meet demand for the Account Mode experience

---

### Scenario B — "Sponsored Spotlight"
**Social media stays dominant + sponsors invest aggressively**

**What's happening:** Fans keep scrolling Instagram and TikTok during dead time, but brands fund in-venue gaming anyway — not as a replacement for social media, but as a guaranteed, measurable spotlight moment social platforms can't offer.

**Impact on us:** Solid, sustainable growth — but engagement volume is lower than Scenario A. Revenue still flows because sponsors value the format itself, even with partial fan attention.

**What we'd need to do:**
- Reframe the sponsor pitch around "guaranteed measurable engagement" rather than "winning back attention from social media"
- Bundle Play the Games into broader sponsorship packages alongside digital/social activations, rather than positioning it as a competitor to social spend
- Double down on data and analytics dashboards — the strongest value proposition in this scenario

---

### Scenario C — "Dead Time Wins"
**Social media stays dominant + sponsors stay conservative**

**What's happening:** The status quo holds. Phones stay in pockets reaching for social media. Sponsors see no proven case for funding something fans haven't visibly demanded, and stick to billboards and jersey logos.

**Impact on us:** The scenario to actively guard against. Without early proof points, the business case stalls before it scales — pilot underperformance here could end the project before Phase 2.

**What we'd need to do:**
- Treat the 2027 Queensland Stadiums pilot as the single most important proof point — over-invest in measurement and reporting
- Secure one anchor sponsor willing to co-invest in proving the concept, even at a loss-leading rate
- Build the lowest-possible-cost MVP so the pilot can run long enough to gather convincing data without burning through capital
- Be ready to pivot the value proposition (e.g. emphasise sport education/inclusion legacy to attract grant or OCOG-aligned funding if commercial sponsor interest is slow)

---

### Scenario D — "Fans Ready, Brands Waiting"
**Fan attention shifts in-venue + sponsors stay conservative**

**What's happening:** Fans are genuinely hungry for something to do during dead time — but sponsors and venues are cautious, unwilling to commit budget to an unproven category.

**Impact on us:** A frustrating but recoverable scenario. The demand-side risk is solved; the supply-side (funding) is the bottleneck.

**What we'd need to do:**
- Run the pilot without depending on large sponsor commitments — use venue-funded or self-funded small-prize models in Guest Mode to prove fan demand first
- Use strong fan engagement metrics as the lead argument to unlock sponsor budget — let demand data do the selling
- Consider interim revenue models (e.g. small participation fees, food/beverage partner co-funding) to bridge the gap until sponsors catch up

---

## Step 4 | Scenario Readiness — The Transferable Principle

Across all four scenarios, the lean, low-cost 2027 pilot is the one strategy that performs reasonably well no matter which future unfolds. It proves fan demand in Scenarios A and D, and it proves commercial viability in Scenarios A and B — giving the team the evidence needed to respond quickly regardless of how the next two uncertainties resolve. This is what builds organisational resilience into the plan from day one, rather than betting everything on a single predicted future.
