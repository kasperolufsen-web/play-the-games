# Quick-Fire Vulnerabilities + How to Defend Them

| Vulnerability | Quick Counter-Argument | Evidence That Would Strengthen It |
|---|---|---|
| Competitors (Monterosa, LiveLike) could copy the QR-flow fast | "We don't need to out-build them everywhere - we need to own the Brisbane 2032-specific moment they aren't purpose-built for." | Evidence incumbents have no Olympic-specific roadmap (e.g. no public Olympic case studies) |
| Incumbents already have venue relationships | "We're not replacing existing sponsor relationships - we add a new inventory layer venues didn't have before." | A venue operator testimonial saying they'd welcome additional inventory, not a competing platform |
| 30,000+ simultaneous users is a hard engineering problem | "That's exactly why the pilot exists - small scale first, stress-test before Olympic load." | A successful load test at a mid-size domestic venue (e.g. 20,000-seat stadium) |
| Venue Wi-Fi could fail publicly | "The pilot phase is designed to surface this risk before it's public at Olympic scale." | Wi-Fi capacity audit/commitment from a partner venue |
| Novelty wears off - will fans still play in year 3? | "Hourly rotating content formats are built specifically to fight novelty fatigue." | Retention/engagement data from a comparable gamified platform over multiple seasons |
| Some fans actively don't want their phone out | "It's fully opt-in - non-participants lose nothing, and we're not forcing engagement." | Survey data showing low complaint/opt-out rates from a pilot |
| Sponsors may see this as cannibalising social media spend, not additive | "In-venue is the one channel social media structurally can't reach - physical presence plus immediate redemption." | A sponsor case study showing in-venue + social spend used together, not traded off |
| "Skill not chance" is doing heavy legal lifting | "This is flagged explicitly as a phase-one legal workstream requiring expert partnership, not solved yet." | A legal opinion letter confirming compliance under Queensland prize/gambling law |
| Data sharing with sponsors raises privacy concerns | "Minimal data collection by design - opt-in, anonymised, capped - built around Privacy Act compliance from day one." | An OAIC-aligned privacy impact assessment |
| Need OCOG + venues + sponsors to all say yes - three-sided dependency | "The phased pilot is how we earn trust incrementally rather than needing it all at once." | Any single signed pilot agreement - even one - proves the model isn't purely theoretical |
| OCOG already has many competing vendor pitches | Hardest one to fully defend - acknowledge directly, lean on the low-cost, low-risk pilot as the differentiator | A successful, documented domestic pilot result before approaching OCOG |
| Post-2032 licensing assumes AFL/NRL/Cricket want an Olympic-built platform | "The domestic pilot in 2027 - before the Olympics - validates the model fits their context first." | Interest/intent expressed by even one domestic code during the pilot phase |

## The One to Be Ready For Above All Others

The financial model stacks three unproven assumptions - revenue share %, fee-per-session pricing, and 30,000-user technical feasibility - on top of each other. The single best answer is always the same: the phased pilot exists specifically to validate each of these before any large commitment is made.
