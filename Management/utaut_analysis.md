# UTAUT Framework Applied to Play the Games

*Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (Venkatesh et al., 2003)*

UTAUT explains why users adopt or reject a new technology through four core determinants. Applying it to Play the Games gives a theoretically grounded answer to "will fans actually use this?" — and shows exactly which levers the platform pulls to maximise adoption.

---

## The Four Core Determinants

### 1. Performance Expectancy
*The degree to which a fan believes using Play the Games will help them get something they value*

**How it applies:** Fans believe the platform will deliver real, tangible value — a redeemable prize, sport knowledge, or entertainment during otherwise wasted dead time.

**How we influence it:**
- Make rewards immediate and visible (instant voucher, not a delayed email)
- Show the live leaderboard on the stadium screen so the "win" feels real and public
- Communicate the value clearly at the QR touchpoint ("Win a free drink in 2 minutes")

---

### 2. Effort Expectancy
*How easy the fan perceives the system to be to use*

**How it applies:** This is the single strongest lever Play the Games has, because the entire design philosophy is built around removing friction.

**How we influence it:**
- No app download, no password — QR scan to play in under 3 seconds
- Guest Mode requires zero registration
- Minimal onboarding (language, section, accessibility only)
- Game sessions are short (2-3 minutes), matching the actual length of dead time
- **Gamification as a deliberate Effort Expectancy strategy:** Game-design elements such as points, leaderboards, badges, and instant feedback loops are not just engagement tools — research shows they actively lower the perceived effort and cognitive load of using a new system, because familiar, intuitive game mechanics make the interaction feel natural rather than like "learning new software" (Sailer & Homner, 2020). More recent work confirms this effect specifically in reward-driven mobile and digital contexts, showing that well-designed gamification continues to reduce perceived complexity and increase ease-of-use even in fast-paced, low-attention environments (Woolley & Sharif, 2025) — exactly the kind of high-distraction, short-window context dead time represents.
- Simple, familiar game formats (quizzes, predictions, leaderboards) lean on mechanics fans already intuitively understand from everyday digital life, minimising the learning curve even for first-time users

This is the determinant Play the Games is most explicitly engineered around — "frictionless by design," reinforced by gamification mechanics, directly targets Effort Expectancy.

---

### 3. Social Influence
*The degree to which a fan perceives that people who matter to them believe they should use the platform*

**How it applies:** Fans are more likely to play if they see others around them playing, or if it feels like a shared, social moment rather than an isolated app.

**How we influence it:**
- Section-based leaderboards create visible peer competition ("Section 114 is winning")
- Stadium screen displays winners publicly — playing becomes a shared spectacle, not a private app interaction
- Family Mode / group play options let friends and family compete together
- Social sharing of wins (built-in share prompts) extends influence beyond the venue

---

### 4. Facilitating Conditions
*The fan's belief that the technical and organisational infrastructure exists to support using the system*

**How it applies:** Fans need confidence that the Wi-Fi will work, the platform will load, and the experience won't fail mid-game.

**How we influence it:**
- Guaranteed venue Wi-Fi access bundled directly into the QR scan flow
- Game runs locally on the device (not server-dependent) to avoid lag with 30,000+ simultaneous users
- Clear on-screen instructions and stadium signage reduce uncertainty before the fan even scans
- Staff/signage support at pilot venues to build initial trust in the system

---

## Moderating Factors — Tailoring the Experience

UTAUT identifies four moderators that change how strongly each determinant matters for different users. These map directly onto the personas already defined for the project.

| Moderator | Relevance to Play the Games |
|---|---|
| Age | Older fans may weight Effort Expectancy and Facilitating Conditions more heavily — needs to feel simple and reliable, not flashy |
| Gender | Less central to this context, but worth noting in broader DEI framing |
| Experience | First-time Olympic viewers (international visitors, first-time sport viewers) will lean on Performance Expectancy — what's in it for me? — more than habitual users |
| Voluntariness of Use | Critical here: Play the Games is entirely opt-in. Voluntariness strengthens the importance of Social Influence and Performance Expectancy, since no one is forced to play — the experience has to sell itself in the moment |

---

## Strategic Takeaway

UTAUT shows that adoption isn't just about having a good idea — it's about deliberately designing for all four determinants simultaneously. Play the Games' core design choices (QR-only entry, no app, instant rewards, public leaderboards, guaranteed Wi-Fi) aren't arbitrary UX decisions — they map directly onto the four UTAUT levers that drive real-world technology adoption.
